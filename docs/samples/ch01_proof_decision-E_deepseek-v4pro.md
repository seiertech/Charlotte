*One snap. Five different reasons.*

Maya's voice cracks at 3pm. She snaps at her son. Her jaw is a hinge with sand in it. She slept five hours. Not because she was busy—because her mind wouldn't stop running on low charge. She isn't angry at him. Her jaw is. The fix isn't a talk. The fix is a pillow.

David's hands snap. He doesn't raise them. He just grips the edge of the desk too hard. His colleague asks a simple question and David's answer comes out sharp, as if the words were already flying before he could catch them. He slept eight hours. He ate lunch. But his eyes flicker. His wiring is fast. The reaction leaves the gate before the decision even puts its shoes on. His body knows he's threatened before his mind knows the question was harmless. The fix isn't sleep. The fix is a half-second pause. A literal pause. One breath. One blink. He learns it later. He'll need to learn it a thousand times.

Lena's eyes narrow at 3pm. She snaps at her partner about the dishes. It's the third time this week. Same time. Same trigger. Same sentence almost. Her stomach tightens beforehand—a little knot she barely notices anymore. The groove is worn so deep the water runs down it without asking. This isn't about today's dishes. This is about Tuesday's and Monday's and last month's. The fix isn't a pause. The fix is noticing the knot. Naming it before it names her.

James walks into a room at 3pm and feels his temperature rise. He hasn't spoken yet. But the air in the room is already hot. Someone before him was anxious. Someone's silence was heavy. James picks it up like a radio signal he didn't tune to. His neck warms. His voice comes out clipped. He snaps at the intern. The intern didn't cause it. The room did. The fix isn't in James' wiring or his sleep. The fix is a question he learns to ask himself: *Is this mine?* It often isn't.

Naomi snaps at her sister at 3pm and it feels almost righteous. Her spine straightens. Her voice is clear and cold. She isn't tired. She isn't patterned. The room is calm. But inside her, a sentence runs on a loop: *I'm the one who holds everything together, so I'm the one allowed to break.* She's been running that story for years. It was a survival story once. Now it's just a script. She snaps because the story demands its release valve. The fix isn't a breath or a question. The fix is a new story, one that doesn't end with her as the necessary breaker.

Five people. One symptom. Five different bodies.

Maya's jaw. David's hands. Lena's stomach. James' neck. Naomi's spine.

Five different reasons. Five different fixes.

Which one was yours?

Maybe more than one. That's the point. The snap is never just the snap. It has a layer. It has a home. It lives somewhere in your system. The work is to find which layer is loudest today. Not to fix all five at once. Just to know: *This time, it's sleep. This time, it's the room. This time, it's the old story running me.*

Knowing is the first repair.

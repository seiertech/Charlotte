**THE DOORWAY**

At 3 p.m., each of them snaps.

The first woman's neck is a taut cable. She slept five hours, and her jaw keeps sliding sideways like a drawer that won't close. When her colleague leans over to ask if she's free for a quick sync, her teeth click together before the words do—*No, not now*—and the air between them goes thin. She doesn't mean to sound sharp, but her body is running on fumes, and fumes have edges.

The second woman is fully rested. Her spine is loose, her fingers warm. But when her partner sets a mug down too loudly, her hand jerks up to shield her face before her mind even registers the sound. The apology comes later—*Sorry, I flinched*—but the damage is done. The reaction fired before the decision arrived, like a spark jumping a gap.

The third woman has done this before. Same day, same time, same sharp inhale before the words spill out. It's Tuesday, and Tuesday at 3 p.m. is when she snaps at her assistant for forgetting to refill the printer. It's Thursday, and Thursday at 3 p.m. is when she snaps at her kid for leaving crumbs on the counter. The groove is worn deep enough that her tongue moves without her. She doesn't even notice the pattern until the apology sticks in her throat—*Why do I keep doing this?*

The fourth woman walks into a room already tense. The air is thick, charged, like the moment before a storm breaks. Her coworker is mid-sentence, voice tight, and before she can think, her own reply is out—*That's not how this works*—sharp as a knife. She didn't bring the weather, but she's running it now. The room exhales when she leaves, but the tension lingers, clinging to her like static.

The fifth woman doesn't snap at the person in front of her. She snaps at the story in her head. *I'm the one who holds everything together, so I'm the one allowed to break.* The words come out aimed at her partner, but they're not really for him. They're for the invisible ledger she carries, the one that says *If I don't do it, no one will.* The snap isn't about the moment—it's about the coat she's been wearing all day, heavy with obligations no one asked her to take on.

Five women. Five bodies. Five different reasons the same thing happened.

The first woman's problem is sleep. The second's is wiring. The third's is habit. The fourth's is room. The fifth's is story.

Which one was yours?

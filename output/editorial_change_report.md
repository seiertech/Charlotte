# Editorial Change Report

*THE SYSTEM OF YOU — Draft 3 → Draft 4*

This report has two parts. **Part A** is the editorial feedback schedule that the editing agents were instructed to apply to the third draft. **Part B** is the detailed schedule of what was actually changed and updated across the book, chapter by chapter, mapped back to the feedback that prompted each change.

---

## Part A — Editorial Feedback Schedule

The rewrite of Draft 3 was driven by two independent pieces of editorial feedback, consolidated into a single governing directive set. The directives below are the exact rules every editing agent was required to read and enforce. They are grouped by theme.

### A.0 — Author Voice Continuity

The author's own voice is the emotional anchor of the book — direct, conversational, lived, unpolished on purpose. Two voices run throughout: the calm mentor who explains the system, and the author who has clearly been through it.

- The Introduction weaves the author's real note directly into the text; it is never rewritten into the mentor voice.
- Every chapter opening carries a beat of the author's warmth and directness before the mentor takes over.
- Every `[AUTHOR VOICE]` insertion point uses the author's register, not the polished mentor voice.
- The author's note is an input to every chapter rewrite, for tone calibration, even where there is no insertion point.

### A.1 — Character Consolidation

Draft 3 suffered from character proliferation — interchangeable one-off names appearing once and vanishing. Draft 4 consolidates to a fixed cast of five recurring characters, each tied to a layer.

- All named characters must come from the Character Bible; no agent may introduce a new named character.
- One-off Draft 3 characters are mapped to the fixed cast by matching primary layer, preserving what happens while changing who.
- Maximum two unnamed background figures per chapter, none carrying dialogue longer than one sentence.
- No character may dominate more than 40% of chapter-level appearances.
- Every character has a distinct physical signature that appears on every appearance.

**The fixed cast:**

| Character | Layer | Physical signature |
|-----------|-------|--------------------|
| Maren | Body | A blur behind the eyes — the soft unfocus that arrives before she knows she's tired |
| Callum | Wiring | A gear-slip in the voice — reaction arriving before decision |
| Grace | Habit | The groove — routine running itself, on autopilot |
| Marcus | Room | A change in the weather of the room — temperature drop, pulled-back quiet |
| Elena | Story | An uninvited sentence arriving behind the eyes |

### A.2 — Repetition Reduction

Both editors flagged pervasive repetition: restated explanations, duplicated content across chapters, and the same metaphors recurring without development.

- De-duplicate across chapters: a concept fully explained once is referenced briefly thereafter, not re-explained.
- Cut restated paragraphs within and across chapters.
- Retire overused phrases. Banned (hard fail): *chest*, *clench(ed)*, *shoulders*, *lands like a stone*, *why can't I just*, *the words swim*.
- Metaphors may recur only if developed — extended, complicated, or subverted — never repeated verbatim.

### A.3 — Production-Artifact Removal

Residual pipeline artifacts must be detected and removed before publication.

| Pattern | Action |
|---------|--------|
| `EDITOR FLAG` markers | Remove |
| `[Author's note]` placeholders | Remove (except in the Introduction, routed to the author) |
| Outline-style purpose headers | Remove |
| Chapter misnumbering | Correct to match the table of contents |
| Internal YAML / workflow code blocks | Remove |
| HTML comments | Remove |
| `TODO` / `FIXME` / `HACK` markers | Remove or resolve |

### A.4 — Author-Presence Insertion Points

The book's credibility depends on the author's presence — not as a character, but as the person behind the system. Draft 4 inserts the author at defined points using `[AUTHOR VOICE]` markers with `[NOTE TO AUTHOR]` briefs. Agents insert the marker and brief but never fabricate autobiographical content.

| Chapter | Type | What is needed | Length |
|---------|------|----------------|--------|
| Introduction | PERSONAL_STORY | Where the model came from; one real before-and-after; who it has helped | 200–500 words |
| The Doorway | VOICE_MOMENT | The first time you saw the layers in yourself | 50–100 words |
| Wiring | VOICE_MOMENT | A personal experience of reacting before thinking | 50–100 words |
| The Method Made Whole | VOICE_MOMENT | A moment the method worked — or honestly failed — for you | 100–200 words |
| The Toolkit / Worked Situations | VOICE_MOMENT | How you discovered you'd been working on the wrong layer | 100–150 words |
| Belief, Then Faith | VOICE_MOMENT | What you hold to when proof isn't there | 100–200 words |
| Growth Map | VOICE_MOMENT | Your own growth map — the book's emotional close | 150–300 words |

### A.5 — Sourcing Requirements

The manuscript needs a credible evidentiary spine without becoming a textbook.

- Every psychological, neurological, behavioural, or health claim stated as fact must carry either a lightweight reference to a well-known source or an honest qualifier ("in my model…", "what the research suggests, broadly…").
- Absolute non-fabrication rule: no invented citations, study names, or publication details.
- Claims that cannot be sourced or qualified are flagged with `[NOTE TO AUTHOR: SOURCE_NEEDED]`.
- References are light — endnote style, never mid-sentence academic parentheticals.

### A.6 — Layer-Boundary Clarifications

Draft 3's layers sometimes blurred. Draft 4 enforces crisp boundaries from the Model Bible.

- Exact layer names only: Body, Wiring, Habit, Room, Story. Exact currency names only: Charge, Fuel, Pressure.
- The Wiring-vs-Story boundary — the most common confusion — is resolved with an explicit discrimination test.
- Each layer has its own sensory grammar (Body: weight, temperature, blur; Wiring: speed, gear-slip; Habit: groove, automaticity; Room: temperature, pull, weather; Story: narration, the uninvited sentence).
- Each layer chapter includes a "commonly mistaken for" section.
- The diagnostic sequence is always bottom-up: Body → Wiring → Habit → Room → Story.

### A.7 — Taxonomy Reframing and Targeted Rewrites

The taxonomy is reframed from a classification system to be memorised into a diagnostic tool to be used. Two chapters received targeted structural rewrites:

**The Hard Cases — five-category constraint taxonomy:**

| Category | Definition | Appropriate response |
|----------|------------|----------------------|
| Immutable | Will not change regardless of action | Adaptation and integration |
| Currently constrained | Fixed now, may change with time or treatment | Patience and management; reassess |
| Externally imposed | A barrier created by systems or others, not the reader's wiring | Challenge the system, seek support — not "adapt internally" |
| Changeable-but-costly | Could change, but at significant personal cost | Honest cost-benefit; name the cost, don't decide for the reader |
| Unsafe | The reader is at risk of harm | Exit, boundary, or safety plan — never adaptation |

**Belief, Then Faith — faith/trust distinction:** the chapter keeps "faith" as its term and adds a paragraph distinguishing faith (acting where evidence runs out) from trust (evidence-based confidence), positioning the word as a deliberate choice, agnostic of religion.

### A.8 — Word-Count Targets

Draft 4 expands the manuscript from roughly 25,000 words toward 33,000–37,000 by cutting repetition while adding substantive new content (mechanism explanations, failure modes, hard cases, counterexamples, model limitations). Permitted expansion is bounded: no new concepts outside the five-layer / three-currency model, no new tools beyond the twelve in the Toolkit, no fabricated research, no clinical advice.

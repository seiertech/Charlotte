# Chapter 1: The Two Anchors

## The proof is in your bones

You didn’t sleep much last night. By the afternoon, something small — a message, a look, a delay — made you far angrier than it should have. It wasn’t really about the small thing. The tiredness came first. It narrowed your patience, and the small thing landed harder than it should have.

You’ve lived that chain a hundred times.

That wired, can’t-switch-off feeling after a poor night’s sleep. The afternoon when something small made you far angrier than it should have. It wasn’t really about the small thing. The tiredness came first. It narrowed everything.

That’s a line of dominoes. Cause, then effect. You’ve lived it a hundred times. This whole book is about learning to see the line — and where to reach into it.

## You are not a mess — you are a system

You are not a motivation problem.
You are not a willpower problem.
You are not a knowledge problem.

You are a **system** — the way your body, your habits, your reactions, and the people around you all work together, whether you notice or not.

You are not a mess.
You are not a mood.
You are not a score out of ten.

You are a real, understandable system. And that changes everything. Because a system can be understood. And a system that can be understood can be changed.

## Nothing is random — everything has a cause

Everything in you runs on cause and effect. Nothing you do is random. Every effect has a cause. And every cause has a **place** you can find in the system.

Find where a problem lives, work at the right spot, in the right order, and it moves.

## The chain you’ve lived

Let’s walk the line you already know.

The tiredness came first. Not the message. Not the look. Not the delay. The tiredness.

It didn’t show up as a grand collapse. It showed up as a narrowing — a smaller space between you and the world. A small thing that would normally slide off now landed harder than it should have.

That’s not weakness.
That’s not a moral failing.
That’s cause and effect.

The tiredness was the cause.
The narrowed patience was the effect.
And the narrowed patience had a place you could find.

## The itch to know where

That line — tiredness to short temper — isn’t random. It’s cause and effect, written in the system you run every day.

The next question is the one that pulls us forward: *Where in you does it live?*

We’ll start with the body you run on — and work our way up from there.

---

## Notice this in you

Take the body first, because it’s the easiest to feel.

**Notice**
How has your energy been this last week? Not good or bad — just, when did it dip, and when did it lift? See if one time of day stands out.

**Trace**
Think back to a moment you snapped, or went foggy, or couldn’t be bothered. What was your body running on just then — enough sleep, or not? Fed, or empty? This is you asking *which layer, what’s it costing me* — for real, about your own day.

**Try**
Pick the one that’s lowest: sleep, food, or movement. Change one small thing tomorrow. To bed twenty minutes earlier. A proper lunch. A ten-minute walk. Small enough that you’ll actually do it.

**Watch**
The day after, notice what shifted. Were you steadier? Slower to snap? Nothing at all? There’s no wrong answer — you’re just gathering what’s true.

**Adjust**
What did that tell you? If it helped, keep it and add nothing yet. If it didn’t, the real dip is probably somewhere else, and you’ve just ruled one thing out. That is progress, not failure.

*One line for the picture of yourself:*
“When my body runs low, the first thing that goes is _______.”
